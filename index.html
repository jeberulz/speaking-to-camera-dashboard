    B-Roll shot list

    Pick one everyday action — making coffee, opening your laptop, walking to your desk, writing notes — and shoot it three ways.

    WIDE

    Whole scene. Where are you? Set the context.

    MEDIUM

    Waist-up / desk-level. The action itself.

    TIGHT

    Hands, face, details. Texture and feeling.

    Try this week: Morning coffee • WeekendMVP desk setup • Whiteboard / notes • Walk & talk • Typing & shipping code

    Talking-head filming checklist

    • Camera at eye level, lens cleaned, look at the lens — not the preview
    • One idea per video, from your idea bank — say it in the first 5 seconds
    • Hook → point → example from building WeekendMVP → close
    • Pause instead of “um” — silence feels longer to you than to viewers
    • Record 2 takes max, keep the better one, move on

    Your daily 10-minute journal

    Every morning in Weeks 1–2 (and as a warm-up after). Unscripted. Unposted. Just you and the lens.

    1. Prop phone/camera up — same spot each day helps
    2. Hit record, talk for 10 minutes about anything: yesterday, WeekendMVP, what you’re learning
    3. Watch it back once — no re-recording
    4. Write your ONE thing for tomorrow below

    B-Roll in One Day

    How to film a month’s worth of B-roll in one day

    By Gabe Harris (@watch.gabe) • 30 Oct 2026 • 7 slides • One focused filming day = a personal stock library you can reuse for a whole month of reels, carousels and shorts.

    Watch the post ↗
    “How to film a month’s worth of B-Roll in one day. If you want to build an ai-empowered content system optimized for quality, comment CHALLENGE to hop on my 30-day challenge wait list.” — caption, instagram.com/p/DfI0cSvoBYL

    The six-step workflow — in order

    1

    Start with a plan

    Make a list of repeatable, useful shots you can film in one space. Working at your desk, drinking coffee, reading or journaling, typing on a laptop, making tea, scrolling on your phone. Build a personal stock library from your real life — don’t invent a fake day.

    2

    Shoot wide, medium & tight

    Film every scene in 3 ways: close-ups add detail and curiosity, mid shots show emotion and action, wide shots set the scene. Combine all 3 for smoother edits and better flow.

    3

    Repeat the action

    Don’t shoot everything from one angle. Break actions into steps and reshoot each step from multiple angles. Walking to your desk = 3–5 steps: pull out laptop, set it down, open it. Mix overhead, POV and low-angle so you can stitch a mini story in post.

    4

    Film your real life

    Turn everyday tasks into content gold: your routine (breakfast, journaling, workouts), your process (editing, brainstorming, behind the scenes), personal moments (books, meditation, slow mornings). Let people into your world — it builds trust fast.

    5

    Don’t forget the stills

    You already have the angle set up — take an extra 60 seconds, set a self-timer and get a still of the same setup. That one habit keeps your carousels and thumbnails on-brand without a separate shoot.

    6

    Organise for reuse

    Build a content bank so you’re never starting from scratch. Create labelled folders on your laptop or phone, back it up on external drives, and reuse clips across reels, carousels and shorts. Consistency gets easier when you’ve already got the footage.

    🗓️ One-day filming plan

    One space, natural light, phone charged, tripod + self-timer ready. Stay in order — routine first while the light and energy are fresh.

    • 30 min
      Plan & set up
      Write your 6 shots, tidy one space, set tripod positions for wide / medium / tight.
    • Block 1
      Routine
      Making tea, drinking coffee, reading / journaling — wide, medium, tight + still each.
    • Block 2
      Desk & process
      Working at your desk and typing on a laptop. Repeat each action step-by-step: sit, open laptop, type, think, ship.
    • Block 3
      Real life & BTS
      Scrolling on your phone, brainstorming / notes, behind-the-scenes of WeekendMVP. Different angles: overhead, POV, low.
    • 20 min
      Stills sweep
      Revisit your best setups, self-timer on, one clean still per scene for carousels & thumbnails.
    • 30 min
      Organise & back up
      File everything into the content bank below, rename clearly, back up — same day, while you remember what’s what.

    🗂️ Content bank — folder structure

    Create this once on your laptop / phone. File by scene first, size second — so in an edit you think “coffee, tight” and go straight to it.

    Content-Bank/ ├─ 01_Desk-Work/ Wide · Medium · Tight · Stills ├─ 02_Coffee-and-Tea/ Wide · Medium · Tight · Stills ├─ 03_Reading-Journaling/ Wide · Medium · Tight · Stills ├─ 04_Typing-Laptop/ Wide · Medium · Tight · Stills ├─ 05_Phone-Scrolling/ Wide · Medium · Tight · Stills ├─ 06_Routine-and-BTS/ Wide · Medium · Tight · Stills ├─ 07_Stills-Carousels-Thumbnails/ └─ _BACKUP/ copy to external drive

    Naming tip: 2026-11_Coffee_Tight_01 — date, scene, size, number. Reuse the same clips across reels, carousels and shorts; that reuse is the whole point.
